The problem

Nine people go into a room. Every one of them has a view. Ninety minutes later there is one view — and it is not necessarily the best one in the room. It is the one everybody could live with.

The first person to speak sets the range everyone else argues inside. The fact known by only one person is often never said. By the time the decision fails, the company has a deck, an outcome and a date — but not the reasoning that led there.

That makes delegation feel dangerous. So the founder or chief executive stays in decisions they should not have to attend, simply because the organisation cannot show what it knew at the time.

The point

The majority decides. The minority survives.

What Triangle changes

It is not a committee and it is not a vote for everything. It is a small, visible structure for consequential decisions.

One clear proposition

The decision is written as one sentence someone can agree or disagree with before the room opens.

The right three people

Participants are chosen for relevant knowledge and consequence, not simply their place on the organisation chart.

A visible position

Each person says Agree or Disagree. Nobody needs to manufacture consensus to get the meeting over with.

A decision that remembers

The result, the minority view, the assumptions and the distribution list remain with the decision.

How a Triangle decision works

The argument remains human. The structure around it stops the important part disappearing.

  1. Issue the proposition in advance.One sentence. Clear enough to agree with or reject. The most important work often happens before anyone begins discussing it.
  2. Name three people and say why.Choose the people who know something material and who will live with the consequence. Write down the reason for each choice.
  3. Let them argue the case.Triangle does not script the conversation or flatten disagreement. It gives the disagreement a defined place to go.
  4. Take final positions privately.Each person submits Agree or Disagree before seeing the others’ final position. That makes following the last speaker harder.
  5. Let two of three carry it.The minority view stays beside the decision, in that person’s own words. It is not softened into meeting minutes.
  6. Keep the record intact.The company records the proposition, the three people, their positions, the result and who may read it. Corrections are appended, not quietly overwritten.

Start with evidence, not belief

Do not roll Triangle out because it sounds right. Test it on decisions the company already has to make.

Every company has a different decision culture. A pilot gives you evidence from your own people, your own decisions and your own operating constraints.

What you commit

One executive sponsor. One decision domain. Four to six real decisions over ninety days. One internal coordinator. Explicit protection for dissent.

What happens

We establish a baseline, facilitate the early decision rooms online, create the records, review the pattern and train an internal steward.

What we test

Whether relevant dissent becomes visible, whether executive involvement becomes more selective, and whether the organisation can reconstruct why it decided.

At the end of ninety days, you decide from evidence: expand, adapt or stop.

How the pilot is delivered

Real decisions. Private online decision rooms. A record the company owns.

The first pilot does not begin with software procurement or a company-wide rollout. We work in a private Teams, Slack or other client-approved decision room. The proposition is issued in advance; the three people discuss it; final positions are submitted privately and revealed together.

The raw conversation remains the source record. Tools can help check completeness and prepare progress reports, but they do not rewrite what people said.

What comes out

  • A baseline view of how decisions currently happen
  • One durable record for each live decision
  • Simple monthly progress reports
  • Measures of process integrity, not people
  • One trained internal steward
  • A final recommendation about wider adoption

What we measure

Not who dissents most. Whether the decision system is becoming more truthful, usable and less dependent on one person.

Visibility of dissent

Was a minority position expressed? Was it retained accurately? Could anyone later pretend that unanimity existed?

Executive dependency

Which eligible decisions still required the CEO? Which were made without them? Were visible overruling decisions necessary?

Organisational memory

Can somebody who was not in the room reconstruct the proposition, assumptions, participants, positions and authority from the record?

Learning over time

Were decisions reopened? Did the same arguments recur? When something changed, could the company see what it had known at the time?

Triangle does not promise better outcomes.

Three people can still be wrong. The promise is more modest and more useful: when a decision is made, the relevant disagreement, judgement and accountability do not vanish with the meeting.

Who it is for

Companies at the point where decisions have outgrown one person’s involvement.

The founder or chief executive cannot be in everything, and knows it. The question is whether they can delegate without becoming blind to the information, dissent and consequence around the decision.

What Triangle is not

  • It does not remove authority. An overrule can still happen — visibly, beside what was known at the time.
  • It is not a performance-management system. Dissent must not become a scorecard.
  • It is not a new committee or a process for every small choice.
  • It is not a promise that three people cannot be confidently wrong together.

Who is behind Triangle

Martin Beyst has spent years coaching senior people at Repsol, Santander, Acciona and Stellantis on how to object when objecting is expensive.

Not confidence training. Not “be more assertive”. Sentence construction under pressure: how to say the difficult thing in a shape that survives the room, and how to stop an objection collapsing into agreement the moment it leaves your mouth.

Triangle came from watching what happens when organisations cannot hear — or cannot keep — what their own people know. The framework is free. The work is helping a company test it honestly on real decisions.
