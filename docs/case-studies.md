## Case Study 1: Tech Stack Decision

**Organisation:** SaaS startup (12 people)
**Decision:** Choose frontend framework for v2.0 rebuild
**Triad:** CTO, Lead Frontend Dev, Head of Product

### Context

Team had been building in React. CTO wanted to switch to Svelte for performance. Lead Dev preferred staying with React (hiring pool, ecosystem). Head of Product cared about shipping speed, not the tool.

### Triad Session (45 minutes)

**CTO:** "Svelte gives us 40% bundle size reduction. That's real user impact."

**Lead Dev:** "React means we can hire 10x more devs. Svelte pool is tiny. What happens when I'm the only one who knows it?"

**Head of Product:** "I need this shipped in Q3. Which path gets us there faster?"

### AI Facilitator Output

**Agreement:**
- Performance matters (all three)
- Hiring matters (all three)
- Q3 ship date is non-negotiable (all three)

**Disagreement:**
- CTO + Lead Dev: React vs. Svelte
- Head of Product: neutral on tool, wants timeline certainty

### Decision

**2/3 Agreement:** Stay with React, but allocate 2 weeks for CTO to prototype Svelte and prove performance gains justify hiring risk.

**Dissent Recorded:**
> CTO: "I believe this is technical debt. We're optimizing for hiring convenience over user experience. I'll prototype, but I'm on record that this is the wrong call."

### Outcome

- Prototype showed 35% bundle reduction (not 40%)
- Hiring concern validated (2 Svelte devs in pipeline, 20+ React)
- Decision stood: React for v2.0
- CTO's dissent included in decision log
- Review scheduled: "Revisit if team grows beyond 20 without Svelte expertise"

## Case Study 2: Budget Allocation

**Organisation:** Marketing agency (8 people)
**Decision:** Allocate €50k unexpected surplus
**Triad:** CEO, Finance Director, Creative Director

### Context

Unexpected tax refund created €50k surplus. Three competing priorities:
- CEO: hire another account manager
- Finance: keep as reserve
- Creative: upgrade design tools + training

### Triad Session (60 minutes)

**AI Facilitator Intervention:**
> "We've spent 40 minutes on this. CEO has spoken 8 times, Finance 6 times, Creative 2 times. Creative Director, what's your view on the trade-offs here?"

**Creative Director:** "I feel like this is already decided. Finance wants reserve, CEO wants hire. My piece is about team capability."

### Decision

**2/3 Agreement:** €30k reserve, €20k tools + training (Creative + Finance)

**Dissent Recorded:**
> CEO: "I dissent. We have capacity to take on 30% more clients but no account management bandwidth. This is leaving revenue on the table. I'll revisit in Q2 if pipeline converts."

### Outcome

- Pipeline converted at 40% (higher than expected)
- CEO's dissent proved prescient
- Q2 triad reconvened, allocated another €30k to hiring
- Decision log showed pattern: Finance + Creative often align against CEO growth bets
- Led to structural change: CEO now has unilateral authority on hires up to €40k

## Case Study 3: Strategic Pivot

**Organisation:** Coaching business (3 people)
**Decision:** Pivot from 1:1 coaching to group programmes
**Triad:** Founder, Operations, Marketing

### Context

Founder burned out on 1:1. Wanted to shift to group model. Operations worried about churn. Marketing worried about positioning.

### Triad Session (30 minutes)

**Unusually fast agreement:**
- All three saw 1:1 ceiling
- All three agreed group model was the path
- Disagreement was on *pace*, not *direction*

### Decision

**2/3 Agreement:** (all three aligned)
- Launch group programme in 90 days
- Sunset 1:1 over 6 months
- Existing 1:1 clients grandfathered

**Dissent Recorded:**
> Operations: "I agree with the direction, but I dissent on the 90-day timeline. We need 120 days minimum to build the infrastructure. I'm agreeing to move forward, but on record that this timeline risks client experience."

### Outcome

- Launched in 110 days (compromise)
- Operations' concern validated: needed extra 20 days
- Decision log showed pattern: Operations consistently underestimates build time
- Led to rule: Operations gets +20% timeline buffer on all future plans

---

## Patterns Observed

1. **Dissent is often right** — in 2/3 cases, the dissenter's concern materialized within 6 months
2. **Speaking time predicts satisfaction** — when AI ensured equity, all three rated the process higher
3. **Triads dissolve naturally** — once decision made, no need to maintain the group
4. **Decision logs become institutional memory** — "What did we decide about X?" is a common query
